IEEE Transactions on Computational Narrative & Algorithmic Game Theory, Vol. 48, No. 3

Computational Analysis of Narrative Convergence in Interactive Digital Media: Central Values and Player Decision Topologies in Branching Visual Novels

Dr. A. Vance1, Prof. K. Morishima2, Dr. S. K. Sterling1
1Laboratory for Statistical Information Dynamics • 2Institute of Behavioral Informatics
Abstract — This study presents a deterministic telemetry framework for analyzing player choice bifurcations and satisfaction metrics within interactive visual novels. By examining statistical distributions of median evaluation scores ($\mathcal{M}_{score} \in [0, 100]$) across $N = 1,420$ narrative corpora, we evaluate the stochastic equilibrium of branch point traversal. Empirical findings suggest that branching complexity correlates strongly with decision dissonance ($\rho = 0.784, p < 0.001$).

1. Methodology & Mathematical Formulation

Let $\mathcal{G} = (V, E)$ denote the directed narrative acyclic graph where $V$ represents narrative nodes and $E$ signifies decision transitions. The median divergence operator is formalized as:

$$\Phi(\mathcal{G}) = \frac{1}{|E|}\sum_{(u, v) \in E} \log_2 \left( 1 + \frac{\sigma(u)}{\sigma(v)} \right) \cdot \oint_{\Gamma} \frac{dz}{z - \lambda_{crit}}$$
